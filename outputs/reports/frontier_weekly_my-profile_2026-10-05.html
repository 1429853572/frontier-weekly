Magnesium fertilization enhances yield and biomass carbon sequestration in global cropping systems: A meta-analysis with field validation in sugar beet Core
Soil and Tillage Research · 2026-10-02 · Meihui Zhao; Baiquan Song; Songlin Yang; Longfeng Wang; Muhammad Ishfaq
Ensuring food security while enhancing agricultural carbon sinks is a central challenge under global climate change. Magnesium is emerging as a key regulator in intensive cropping systems and acidic soils, yet a comprehensive framework linking photosynthetic performance to biomass carbon allocation at the field scale remains lacking. To elucidate the synergistic enhancement of crop production and carbon sequestration capacity by magnesium fertilizer, we integrated a global meta-analysis of 2487 paired observations (1964–2025) with three-year field experiments across six locations in China using sugar beet ( Beta vulgaris L.) as a model root crop. The meta-analysis showed that magnesium application increased crop plants yield by an average of 22.45% globally, with substantial improvements in net photosynthetic rate ( Pn , +32.16%) and photosystem photosynthetic performance index (PI ABS , +44.50%). Magnesium application altered carbon allocation patterns, increasing the root-to-shoot ratio (R/S) and biomass carbon storage (BCS) by 20.84%. To verify these global patterns, multi‑site field validation experiments showed that magnesium application (50–100 kg ha −1 ) persistently improved root yield and BCS of sugar beet across sites. The optimum rate of 80 kg magnesium ha −1 increased BCS by 34.32% relative to low‑magnesium conventional management, whereas excessive magnesium (>100 kg ha −1 ) weakened these benefits. We propose a mechanistic framework of “photosystem activity—carbon assimilation—carbon allocation” driven by magnesium nutrition. Our findings suggest that site‑specific magnesium management within the range of 50–100 kg ha −1 may serve as a promising agronomic strategy for synergistically improving field crops productivity and agronomic carbon sequestration potential, providing important insights for sustainable crop production.
Comparing soil-health- and species-based sensitivity distributions for metal risk assessment in Tianjin soils Core
iScience · 2026-10-01 · Qiuyun Xu; Yajuan Shi; Xin Xiong; Li Qian; Liuxuan Fang et al.
Soil contamination threatens ecosystem functioning, while conventional ecological risk assessment (ERA) based on single-species toxicity may not capture system-level responses. We developed a probabilistic soil health sensitivity distribution (SHSD) framework integrating microcosm-derived soil health indicators with joint probability modeling and applied it to Pb, Cu, and Cd risks in Tianjin soils, northern China. SHSD-derived risks were lower than species sensitivity distribution (SSD)-based estimates by 61.90%, 59.55%, and 84.38% for Pb, Cu, and Cd, respectively. Normalizing SSD toxicity data to microcosm soil conditions reduced part of the SSD-SHSD discrepancy, highlighting the influence of soil physicochemical heterogeneity. Observed SHSDs were also shifted rightward relative to additive, non-interactive predictions, indicating lower apparent sensitivity of the integrated microcosm. These findings support SHSD as a higher tier, function-oriented complement to conventional SSDs, while emphasizing the need for independent field validation before regulatory application.
Niche differentiation and functional complementarity between rare and abundant taxa sustain grassland soil bacterial diversity Core
Communications Earth & Environment · 2026-09-30 · Xiaojuan Zhang; Long Lv; Saman Herath; Jie Yang; Zhuangsheng Tang
Soil bacterial communities are central to grassland ecosystem functioning, yet how abundant and rare bacteria taxa differ in ecological strategies, community formation, and environmental responses remains unclear. Here we show, using 278 soil samples from grasslands across China, that these groups have contrasting but complementary ecological roles. Abundant taxa are mainly adapted to nutrient-poor conditions, tolerate a wider range of environments, and are shaped largely by random ecological processes. Rare taxa are more often associated with nutrient-rich conditions, occupy narrower environmental niches, and are governed more strongly by environmental selection. Machine-learning analyses identify pH and aridity index as the main drivers of diversity and community assembly in both groups. Predicted functions also differ: abundant taxa contribute more to carbon fixation, whereas rare taxa play greater roles in nitrogen cycling. Abundant bacterial functions depend mainly on geography and community composition, whereas rare bacterial functions rely more on diversity and respond more strongly to environmental change, highlighting their complementary contributions. Abundant and rare soil bacteria in Chinese grasslands show contrasting trophic strategies, assembly processes, and functions that underpin bacterial biodiversity, with rare taxa more environmentally sensitive and soil pH regulating both groups, based on analyses of 278 samples and machine learning.
Limosilactobacillus reuteri for oral mucositis in patients undergoing radiotherapy for malignant head and neck tumors: a randomized controlled trial Core
Nature Communications · 2026-09-29 · Kun Gao; Yuan-Tai Li; Yiyan Pei; Zhihui Li; Jia-Gang He et al.
Abstract Oral mucositis (OM) is a major toxicity in patients with head and neck cancer undergoing radiotherapy. Here we conducted a double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized phase II trial (NCT06285591) from March 2024 to January 2025 at West China Hospital of Sichuan University to evaluate whether Limosilactobacillus reuteri ( L. reuteri ) supplementation could reduce severe OM (SOM; WHO Oral Toxicity criteria grade ≥3). A total of 160 patients were randomly assigned to receive L. reuteri probiotic lozenges or placebo twice daily during radiotherapy. The primary endpoint was the incidence of SOM, and secondary endpoints included SOM onset and duration, as well as the incidence, onset, and duration of any-grade OM. Safety outcomes were monitored throughout the study. The primary endpoint was met, with a lower incidence of SOM in the L. reuteri group than in the placebo group (37.5% vs 61.3%; relative risk 0.61; 95% confidence interval 0.44-0.85; absolute risk reduction 23.8 percentage points, 95% CI 8.7% to 38.8%; P = 0.004). L. reuteri supplementation also delayed SOM onset and shortened SOM duration. No significant safety concerns were observed. These findings indicate that L. reuteri supplementation reduces SOM incidence and severity in patients with head and neck cancer receiving radiotherapy.
Global potential and synergistic benefits of soil amendments for saline-alkali soil amelioration, soil carbon sequestration, and plant productivity: A meta-analysis Core
Geoderma · 2026-09-29 · Ruizhe Yang; Tao Jianguo; Qianyuan Duan; Hongwu Zhou; Lichao Tang et al.
Saline-alkali land is a critical arable land reserve, but severe soil salinity-alkalinity threatens ecosystem health and agricultural production security. Organic amendment (OA), inorganic amendment (IA), and organic-inorganic composite amendment (OIA) have been widely used for saline-alkali land rehabilitation, but their effectiveness and synergistic effects in alleviating soil salinity-alkalinity, increasing soil organic carbon (SOC) and plant productivity remain debated. To address these knowledge gaps, based on 3763 observations from 215 studies, we combined a global meta-analysis with machine learning-based spatial prediction models to quantify the effects of OA, IA, and OIA on soil electrical conductivity (EC), pH, SOC content, plant productivity and their comprehensive improvement benefits (CIBs), identify their key drivers, compare the rehabilitation performance of different soil amendments under key environmental factors and future climate scenarios, and map the global distribution of optimal rehabilitation strategies for saline-alkali land. The results showed the OA, IA, and OIA were all effective rehabilitation measures, significantly reducing soil EC and pH and increasing SOC content and plant productivity, with CIBs of 34.7%, 27.5%, and 31.2%, respectively. The effectiveness of soil amendments was driven by initial soil factors, especially soil salinity-alkalinity, C:N ratio, and soil texture. Among soil amendments, OIA was better suited to rehabilitating severely salinized soils. OA and OIA were more favorable for SOC sequestration in high initial C:N ratio soils and had greater synergistic effects for SOC sequestration and plant productivity in sandy and clayey soils, while reducing dependence on irrigation and mineral N inputs. IA showed no advantage over OA or OIA across major initial soil factors, with its rehabilitation benefits requiring irrigation and higher N input rates. Global spatial predictions showed that OA and OIA had broader applicability, and their CIBs remained higher and more stable than those of IA under future climate scenarios. These results highlight the need to prioritize soil amendments involving organic inputs (OA or OIA) in rehabilitation strategies to synergistically achieve soil amelioration, soil carbon sink enhancement, and food security.
Anthropogenic and Geogenic Factors Affecting Groundwater Arsenic and Potential Drinking Water Exposure in the San Luis Valley, Colorado Core
Environmental Science & Technology · 2026-09-29 · Ryan Smith; Alandra Marie Lopez; Alexandder S. Honeyman; Dawson Carney; Scott E. Fendorf et al.
Abstract Groundwater arsenic is a serious health threat in many regions with limited surface water drinking supplies. In the San Luis Valley, Colorado, USA, where most residents rely on groundwater for drinking water, elevated groundwater arsenic concentrations (≥5 μg/L) were observed in 19.8% of private wells. Many recent studies have leveraged data science methods to estimate the spatial variability of arsenic at local, regional, and global scales. These studies typically predict depth-independent arsenic concentrations due to limited well depth information. However, arsenic concentrations may vary greatly with depth due to heterogeneous aquifer geochemistry, including redox potential, pH, and biogeochemical conditions. In this study, we apply random forest modeling to predict elevated groundwater arsenic concentrations (≥5 μg/L) over space and depth continuously across the San Luis Valley using a community-driven data set of 435 groundwater measurements of major ions and trace metals. Our model reveals the influence of several known or hypothesized drivers or proxy drivers of arsenic in groundwater, including surface elevation, fraction of fine-grained aquifer sediments, well depth, soil pH, long-term subsidence, and geothermal influences. Elevation, percentage of fine-grained sediments, and elemental indicators of geothermal fluid influence are some of the most important predictors of arsenic, while long-term subsidence is found to have a weak but positive relationship with arsenic. The model has a specificity of 0.82 and a sensitivity of 0.81 on held-out test data sets. In addition to improving understanding of drivers of arsenic in the region, this model produces pseudo-3D estimates of groundwater arsenic. The model can also be used to assess the time-dependent exposure to arsenic as the depth of domestic wells changes over time.
Tillage and amendment management in organic farm on salt-affected soils in Mediterranean polder-type agricultural land in Croatia Core
Soil and Tillage Research · 2026-09-28 · Igor Bogunović; Kristina Kljak; Paulo Pereira; Marcos Francos; Ivica Kisić et al.
Salt-affected soils in the Mediterranean are difficult to reclaim under organic farming, where amendment options are limited, and hydrology strongly controls salt redistribution. In an oat–triticale–barley–oat rotation, we evaluated different amendments (control, gypsum – G3, gypsum plus sulphur – G3S1, G6S2, gypsum plus farmyard manure (FYM) – G6 + OM, and gypsum plus sulphur plus FYM – G3S1 + OM) and tillage (discing and discing plus ripping) interventions on a saline–sodic Anthrosol in the Raša valley, Croatia. Crop yields were recorded annually, and soil physical and chemical properties were determined at the beginning and end of the trial. Gypsum significantly increased exchangeable Calcium (Ca) and reduced exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP). At the end of the experiment, electrical conductivity (EC) decreased significantly. Water-stable aggregates (WSA) increased significantly in all plots with added amendments, while the control plots showed the poorest structure. Ripping generally lowered bulk density and penetration resistance compared with discing, but effects were treatment- and depth-specific. Yield benefits became most evident in the last two years: barley yield was significantly lower in the control than in all amended treatments, and oat yield was 26–50% higher in G6S2, G3S1 + OM, and G6 + OM than in the control. Overall, the results indicate that gypsum-based amendments, particularly when combined with FYM, can progressively improve soil condition and crop performance under organic management, while the benefits of deep loosening remain more variable and dependent on seasonal soil conditions.
Legacy effects of long-term bird droppings input promoted the activation of soil inorganic phosphorus and associated microorganisms in coastal wetlands Core
CATENA · 2026-09-28 · Haijing Yuan; Xuexin Shao; Keyi Jiang; Jing Xiong; Ming Wu
Coastal wetland reclamation alleviates land scarcity and provides wading bird habitats, yet the legacy effects of bird droppings on soil phosphorus (P) cycling remain poorly understood. Here, we conducted an in situ incubation experiment in Hangzhou Bay coastal wetlands to investigate the legacy effects of long-term (12 years), continuous, and short-term (3 years) bird droppings inputs on soil P fraction transformation. We observed that bird droppings promoted soil inorganic P activation, increasing exchangeable P (Exch-P) and iron‑aluminium bound P ((Fe + Al)-P) by 349.45% and 189.26%, respectively, while decreasing calcium bound P (Ca-P) by 50.00%. Bird droppings also enhanced soil nutrients (SOC, TN, and TP), microbial functions (alkaline phosphatase activity and bacterial richness), and their correlations with Exch-P, (Fe + Al)-P, and organic P. The near - neutral pH of droppings weakened the soil alkaline environment, provided a more suitable growth environment for P activating microorganisms, and increased the negative correlation between pH and Exch-P and the positive correlation between pH and Ca-P. Notably, legacy effects showed a greater impact on the soil than current short-term droppings inputs, and topsoil (0–10 cm) showed stronger responses than subsoil (10–20 cm). The reduction rate of P was faster than that of SOC and TN, which could increase the risk of P loss. This study provides mechanistic understanding of how bird droppings drive soil P dynamics, a public concern, which is vital for sustainable management of P deficient coastal reclaimed wetlands during the “grain-oriented” reclamation.

Feasibility and reliability of farmer-recorded plant diversity indicators in European grasslands Proxy
Agriculture Ecosystems & Environment · 2026-10-03 · Tom Staton; Markus Wagner; Kate Farley; Adrian Gliga; Mignon Şandor et al.
European grasslands are biodiversity hotspots but face ongoing threats from intensification and abandonment. Monitoring is essential for evaluating policies that promote grassland biodiversity, but conventional expert-led surveys are costly and difficult to scale. Farmer-led biodiversity recording offers a potential solution, but its feasibility and reliability remain uncertain. This study aimed to assess whether farmer-recorded indicators can provide reliable and practical measures of grassland plant diversity that could support broader biodiversity monitoring and assessment of public goods delivery. We firstly reviewed 14 European monitoring case study schemes, supplemented by stakeholder interviews in eight of these schemes. Secondly, we compared simple farmer-recorded indicators at the field and farm system levels with expert botanical assessments across 26 low-intensity farms in the UK, Sweden, and Romania. Analysis of case study monitoring schemes revealed that limited time and technical expertise were major barriers to farmer participation, while adequate incentives, scheme simplicity, and supportive technology acted as key enablers alongside the well-documented need for training and support. Comparisons with botanical assessments showed that simple farmer-recorded measures, particularly forb species counts, were strong proxies for plant species richness and diversity, while some abundance-based measures such as vegetation cover estimates also showed significant relationships with botanist assessments. In contrast, farmer-recorded indicator species performed poorly, likely due to short indicator species lists and the lack of farmer training. Farm system metrics were also weak predictors of species richness and diversity, although they may be informative across broader gradients of management intensity. Overall, our findings demonstrate that farmer-led biodiversity recording is feasible when based on straightforward, observable indicators, combined with adequate motivation. These approaches could complement expert assessments in supporting biodiversity goals and results-based agri-environment schemes.

High risk cases are typically referred for stem cell transplantation (SCT) in first remission, but because SCT is associated with substantial toxicity and cures after relapse are rare, accurate risk prediction is critical. Multidimensional Cardiac Phenotyping of Daphnia magna Enables One-Hour Effect-Based Screening of Aquatic Toxicity Proxy
Environmental Science & Technology · 2026-10-02 · Zitong Liao; Zhuo Chen; Yun Lu; Dongbin Wei; Xian‐Wei Liu et al.
Abstract Operational water quality management relies on physicochemical monitoring and targeted chemical analysis, but routine surveillance covers only a prioritized subset of high-risk contaminants and does not capture biological activity from unmonitored chemicals, transformation products, or complex mixtures. Whole-organism bioassays provide complementary effect information, yet the conventional Daphnia magna (D. magna) acute immobilization end point requires 48 h. Here, we introduce D-HEART (Daphnia-based Heartbeat Evaluation and Analysis for Rapid Toxicity Screening), a microfluidic imaging and analysis framework that converts high-speed cardiac recordings into 116 descriptors spanning morphology, pumping, wall motion kinematics, and synchrony. Within the tested response space, D-HEART assigns cardiac-response classes using a six-contaminant reference library, while its Heartbeat Toxicity Index (HTI) estimates 48 h immobilization from the predicted class and phenotype. Transcriptomic profiles provided molecular context for differentiated 1 h cardiac states. Post-training Cd(II) tests across environmental water matrices tracked immobilization with greater uncertainty than in laboratory water, and two untreated industrial wastewaters showed dose-responsive HTI estimates for mixed exposures from unseen contaminant classes. By linking early physiological states to transcriptional responses and later apical toxicity, D-HEART provides a rapid, cross-time scale effect-screening layer for operational water-quality evaluation, reducing the detection window by approximately 98% relative to the conventional 48 h immobilization end point.

Environmental Science & Technology · 2026-10-02 · Rui Ma; Yifeng Zhang; Xiaodong Zheng; Xiang Li; Lan Wei et al.
Abstract Straw and biochar additions can enhance long-term carbon storage, yet how their interactions regulate carbon mineralization and allocation remains unclear. We conducted a 360-day microcosm incubation employing a reciprocal 13C-labeling design that paired 13C-labeled straw with unlabeled biochar and unlabeled straw with 13C-labeled biochar and used single-cell Raman spectroscopy and 13C-labeled cell metagenomics. At day 360, biochar reduced cumulative straw-derived 13CO2 from 1391 to 1199 mg C kg–1 soil, while increasing straw-C allocation to fulvic acid (FA) and humic acid (HA) fractions. Overall, the biochar–straw interaction redirected exogenous carbon from rapid mineralization toward humic substance associated soil organic matter fractions, thereby enhancing soil carbon accrual.

Remodeling mechanisms of large-scale photovoltaic arrays on the plant-soil-microbe system and multifunctionality in alpine grasslands Proxy
Agriculture Ecosystems & Environment · 2026-10-01 · zongcheng zong cai; Fayi Li; Yunfei Xing; Qingqing Liu; Shancun Bao et al.
The cascading pathways through which large-scale photovoltaic (PV) arrays reshape plant-soil-microbe systems and soil nutrient multifunctionality (SNMF) in alpine grasslands remain largely elusive. Based on a field investigation at a Qinghai-Tibet Plateau PV station, we delineated five microhabitats: unshaded control (CK), front-of-panel eaves (FP), underneath panels (UP), rear-eaves (RP), and inter-panel (IP). Integrating vegetation surveys, amplicon sequencing, and structural equation modeling (SEM), we revealed significant PV-induced habitat differentiation. Water-enriched FP zones promoted vegetation growth (density LRR = 0.63; height LRR = 0.25) and maximized SNMF (0.86). Conversely, persistent shading in UP zones caused severe substrate limitation, drastically reducing aboveground biomass (LRR = −0.60), microbial biomass phosphorus (LRR = −0.38), and SNMF (0.18). Under stringent physical filtering, UP microbial diversity declined (bacterial and fungal Shannon indices fell by 4.7% and 13.6%, respectively), and cross-kingdom co-occurrence networks simplified (average degrees dropped by 56.5% and 73.2%, respectively; robustness decreased by more than 58%). Microbial community composition shifted toward specific taxa frequently associated with resource-limited conditions (e.g., the relative abundance of Arthrobacter increased to 3.9%), alongside an accumulation of putative plant pathogens (16.3%), signaling localized ecological vulnerability. Structural equation modeling suggested a potential pathway where PV microhabitats determined SNMF primarily by reshaping plant phenotypes. Vegetation acted as the central hub (total effect = 0.78), directly driving SNMF (β = 0.63) and regulating bacterial diversity (β = 0.37). Ultimately, shading-induced vegetation decline and substrate limitation drive sub-panel microecological constraints during the peak growing season. Spatially targeted management (e.g., introducing shade-tolerant grasses under panels) is essential to restore plant-mediated positive feedbacks for sustainable solar-pastoral systems.

Polyethylene, polypropylene, and polyurethane were the predominant MP markers. As MP accumulation increased, microbial carbon limitation was alleviated, whereas widespread microbial phosphorus limitation was prevalent. Overall, this study demonstrates that MP accumulation induces nutrient stoichiometric imbalances, thereby reshaping microbial community assembly and nutrient cycling, and provides new insight into the ecological risk assessment of plastic-mulched farmland.
Toxicokinetics Reveal Structure-Related Differences in Developmental Toxicity among Typical Aromatic Disinfection Byproducts in Zebrafish Embryos Proxy
Environmental Science & Technology · 2026-10-01 · Xin Li; Guifang Pan; Ling Zhang; Huizhen Li; Jing You
Abstract Aromatic disinfection byproducts (DBPs) are emerging contaminants generated during drinking water disinfection and exhibit higher cytotoxicity and genotoxicity than traditional aliphatic DBPs. However, in vivo toxicokinetic behavior of aromatic DBPs during sensitive developmental stages remains poorly understood, limiting accurate risk assessment. Here, five aromatic DBPs within three classes (halophenols, halonitrophenol, and halohydroxybenzaldehydes) were systematically investigated using zebrafish embryos to compare developmental toxicity and characterize toxicokinetics. Developmental toxicity tests showed that the median lethal concentrations (LC50) of halonitrophenol were lower than halophenols and halohydroxybenzaldehydes, and brominated analogues were consistently more toxic than chlorinated counterparts. When expressed as median lethal residues (LR50) instead of LC50, toxicity differences decreased from 13-fold to 3-fold, indicating that reliance on external concentrations may misrepresent toxic potency. Toxicokinetic analysis showed that halophenol and halonitrophenol DBPs exhibited higher uptake and slower elimination, resulting in appreciable bioaccumulation and elevated internal exposure. In contrast, halohydroxybenzaldehydes exhibited substantially lower uptake rate constants, whereas elimination rate constants were 2–3 orders of magnitude higher, collectively resulting in extremely low bioaccumulation and negligible internal residues. These distinct kinetic profiles provide, for the first time among aromatic DBPs, insights linking chemical structure, internal exposure, and toxicity, supporting more biologically relevant risk-based prioritization.

Abstract Microbial ammonia oxidation, the first and rate-limiting step of nitrification, plays a central role in soil nitrogen cycling. It is most relevant in agricultural soils as nitrifiers compete with crops for ammonia-based fertilizers.
